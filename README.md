# Mathematics Notes

This repository is my personal collection of notes, derivations, proofs, calculations, problems, and short expositions on pure and applied mathematics. I use it as a continuously evolving record of subjects that I study, revisit, teach, or simply consider worth exploring in greater detail. It is not intended to reproduce the structure of a textbook or a conventional university course. I organize the repository according to canonical branches of mathematics, while individual topics are developed as independent and largely self-contained notes.

Depending on the subject, a note may include definitions, propositions, theorems, proofs, worked problems, analytical calculations, numerical experiments, figures, references, computational code, and complementary material. Some notes may remain elementary and concise, whereas others may develop into more extensive mathematical expositions. My emphasis is on explicit reasoning, rigorous derivations, mathematical structure, clear notation, and reproducibility.

## Repository Organization

```text
notes_maths/
├── elementary_mathematics/
├── algebra/
├── analysis/
├── geometry/
├── topology/
├── number_theory/
├── combinatorics/
├── logic_and_foundations/
├── numerical_analysis/
├── optimization/
└── templates/
```

Within each discipline, I organize individual subjects as independent and largely self-contained notes. A note may contain its own LaTeX source, figures, references, computational code, data, or other assets whenever required. Directory and file names are written in English and follow a simple `snake_case` convention whenever practical.

I do not impose a rigid internal structure across the repository. A folder such as `analysis/legendre_polynomials`, for example, should be understood as an autonomous mathematical note rather than as a chapter in a predetermined sequence. New subjects can therefore be added without requiring a global reorganization of the repository.

I intend this repository to function as a mathematical notebook rather than a finished textbook. Elementary problems, classical theorems, proofs, computational experiments, advanced topics, and research-oriented ideas may coexist and evolve independently. The classification into disciplines serves primarily as a navigational structure; mathematical subjects naturally overlap, and individual notes may connect ideas from several areas.

Probability and statistics are maintained separately in my statistics repository, while mathematical methods developed primarily for physical applications belong to my physics repositories. This separation keeps the present repository centered on mathematics itself.
